PERSONAL CORRESPONDENCE · NOVEMBER 25, 2008

Nathaniel,

I have known you too long to accept what is happening between us. The work behind the Langford Award meant something. We used to argue over a result until we could both stand behind it.

Now you ask for confidence while keeping the original measurements out of reach. When I ask questions, you speak as though our friendship gives you permission to put my reputation at risk.

I will not sign my name to an account I cannot verify. Nor will I accept responsibility for work that was changed after I left the project. This is not the man I thought I knew.

We should speak privately when I see you on Thanksgiving. I would rather settle this face to face than through another carefully worded letter.
